# 2. Architecture Vision

InvoicePro is designed as a cloud-native, multi-tenant Software-as-a-Service platform that enables businesses of every size to manage invoicing and business operations from a single application.

The architecture prioritizes simplicity for users while maintaining flexibility, scalability, security, and maintainability for engineering teams.

Every architectural decision should support long-term growth without requiring fundamental redesign as the platform evolves from serving individual businesses to enterprise organizations.

The platform is designed to grow incrementally, allowing businesses to begin with basic invoicing and progressively adopt more advanced capabilities over time.

# 4. Architecture Principles

## Overview

Architecture Principles define the fundamental engineering rules that govern every technical decision within InvoicePro.

These principles ensure consistency across the platform regardless of team size, technology stack, or future architectural evolution.

Every new feature, module, API, database schema, integration, and infrastructure component must align with these principles.

When architectural trade-offs arise, these principles serve as the primary decision-making framework.

---

# AP-001 — Business First

## Principle

Technology exists to solve business problems.

Architecture decisions should always prioritize business value over technical elegance.

## Why

InvoicePro is built for businesses, not developers.

Every feature should improve operational efficiency, reduce manual work, or help businesses grow.

## Implementation

- Business requirements drive architecture.
- Technology choices support business goals.
- Avoid unnecessary complexity.

---

# AP-002 — Simplicity Over Complexity

## Principle

Prefer the simplest solution that solves the problem correctly.

## Why

Simple systems are easier to understand, maintain, test, and scale.

Complexity should only be introduced when justified by measurable business value.

## Implementation

- Avoid premature optimization.
- Minimize unnecessary abstractions.
- Keep user workflows intuitive.
- Reduce cognitive load for developers.

---

# AP-003 — API First

## Principle

Every business capability should be designed as an API before implementation.

## Why

API-first architecture enables consistent communication between web applications, mobile applications, third-party integrations, and future public APIs.

## Implementation

- APIs define business contracts.
- Frontend consumes APIs.
- Mobile applications consume the same APIs.
- Future integrations reuse existing APIs.

---

# AP-004 — Multi-Tenant by Design

## Principle

Every architectural decision must support multi-tenancy.

## Why

InvoicePro is a SaaS platform where multiple organizations share the same infrastructure while remaining completely isolated.

## Implementation

- Organization isolation is mandatory.
- Data ownership is explicit.
- Tenant boundaries are enforced at every layer.
- Cross-tenant access is prohibited.

---

# AP-005 — Security by Design

## Principle

Security is integrated into architecture rather than added later.

## Why

Security failures damage customer trust and business reputation.

## Implementation

- Authentication required.
- Authorization enforced.
- Principle of least privilege.
- Encryption in transit.
- Encryption at rest where applicable.
- Secrets never stored in source code.

---

# AP-006 — Stateless Services

## Principle

Application servers should remain stateless whenever possible.

## Why

Stateless services simplify scaling, deployment, failover, and load balancing.

## Implementation

- Sessions stored securely outside application memory.
- APIs remain independent.
- Horizontal scaling supported.

---

# AP-007 — Domain Ownership

## Principle

Each business domain owns its own responsibilities.

## Why

Clear ownership reduces coupling and simplifies future expansion.

## Examples

Invoice Domain owns:

- Invoice creation
- Invoice lifecycle
- Invoice numbering

Payment Domain owns:

- Payment recording
- Outstanding balances
- Payment reconciliation

Reporting Domain consumes data but never owns it.

---

# AP-008 — Event-Driven Communication

## Principle

Business events should notify interested systems without creating tight coupling.

## Why

Loose coupling improves scalability, maintainability, and extensibility.

## Examples

Invoice Created

↓

Generate PDF

↓

Send Email

↓

Create Notification

↓

Update Analytics

↓

Create Audit Log

Each operation reacts independently to the business event.

---

# AP-009 — Single Source of Truth

## Principle

Every business entity has one authoritative owner.

## Why

Duplicate ownership leads to inconsistent data.

## Examples

Customer Name

Owned by Customer Module.

Invoice stores only a snapshot required for historical accuracy.

Product Pricing

Owned by Product Module.

Invoice captures pricing at the time of creation.

---

# AP-010 — Fail Gracefully

## Principle

Failures should degrade functionality without corrupting business data.

## Why

Unexpected failures are inevitable.

Users should recover quickly.

## Implementation

- Friendly error messages.
- Retry where appropriate.
- Preserve user input.
- Log failures.
- Prevent data corruption.

---

# AP-011 — Observability by Default

## Principle

Every important operation should be measurable.

## Why

Systems cannot be improved if they cannot be observed.

## Implementation

- Structured logging.
- Metrics.
- Health checks.
- Audit logs.
- Analytics events.
- Distributed tracing (Future).

---

# AP-012 — Performance First

## Principle

Performance is considered during design, not after deployment.

## Why

Slow systems reduce user productivity and customer satisfaction.

## Implementation

- Efficient database queries.
- Pagination.
- Background processing.
- Caching where appropriate.
- Lazy loading.
- Optimized APIs.

---

# AP-013 — Scalability by Default

## Principle

Architecture decisions should support long-term growth.

## Why

InvoicePro should evolve from serving hundreds of organizations to hundreds of thousands without architectural redesign.

## Implementation

- Horizontal scaling.
- Queue-based processing.
- Independent services.
- Modular architecture.
- Infrastructure automation.

---

# AP-014 — Modularity

## Principle

Every module should have a clearly defined responsibility.

## Why

Independent modules simplify maintenance and future development.

## Implementation

Modules communicate through defined interfaces.

Business logic remains isolated.

Shared functionality is reusable.

---

# AP-015 — Documentation Driven Development

## Principle

Architecture decisions should be documented before implementation.

## Why

Documentation preserves knowledge, reduces ambiguity, and accelerates onboarding.

## Implementation

Major technical decisions require:

- Architecture documentation
- Business reasoning
- Trade-off analysis
- Future considerations

---

# Architecture Review Checklist

Every architectural decision should answer:

- Does it solve a business problem?
- Does it follow these principles?
- Is it scalable?
- Is it secure?
- Is it maintainable?
- Is it observable?
- Is it modular?
- Can it evolve without major redesign?

If the answer to any question is "No", the decision should be re-evaluated.

# Architectural Style

InvoicePro follows a Modular Monolith Architecture.

---

## Why Modular Monolith?

Benefits

- Faster Development
- Simpler Deployment
- Lower Operational Complexity
- Easier Testing
- Lower Infrastructure Cost

---

## Why Not Microservices?

Current Scale Does Not Require It

Microservices would introduce:

- Service Discovery
- Distributed Transactions
- Operational Overhead
- Monitoring Complexity

without delivering meaningful business value.

# 6. Core Business Domains

## Overview

InvoicePro is organized around business domains rather than technical layers.

A domain represents a specific business capability with clear ownership, responsibilities, rules, and data boundaries.

Every domain owns its own business logic and data.

Domains communicate through well-defined interfaces and events rather than directly modifying each other's data.

This approach promotes:

- Scalability
- Maintainability
- Separation of Concerns
- Independent Evolution
- Clear Ownership

---

# Domain Design Principles

Every domain must:

- Have a clearly defined responsibility.
- Own its own business rules.
- Own its own data.
- Expose defined interfaces.
- Avoid direct database access across domains.
- Communicate through services and events.

# 8. Request Lifecycle

## Overview

The Request Lifecycle defines how every request travels through InvoicePro from the moment a user initiates an action until a response is returned.

A consistent request lifecycle ensures:

- Predictable behavior
- Strong security
- Consistent validation
- Reliable auditing
- Proper observability
- Easier debugging

Every business operation should follow this lifecycle.

---

# Lifecycle Principles

Every request must:

- Be authenticated
- Be authorized
- Be validated
- Execute business logic
- Persist data safely
- Publish events
- Generate observability data
- Return standardized responses

---

# High-Level Flow

```text
User Action
      ↓
Client Application
      ↓
API Endpoint
      ↓
Authentication
      ↓
Authorization
      ↓
Request Validation
      ↓
Business Service
      ↓
Database Transaction
      ↓
Domain Event
      ↓
Response
      ↓
Background Processing
```

---

# Example Request

User clicks:

Create Invoice

---

# Step 1 — Client Request

The Web Application sends a request to the API.

Example

```http
POST /api/v1/invoices
```

Responsibilities:

- Collect user input
- Perform basic client validation
- Attach access token
- Send request

---

# Step 2 — API Layer

The API Layer receives the request.

Responsibilities:

- Route resolution
- Request context creation
- Correlation ID generation
- Logging initialization

Example Context

```json
{
  "requestId": "req_123",
  "organizationId": "org_456",
  "userId": "usr_789"
}
```

---

# Step 3 — Authentication

Purpose

Verify the user's identity.

Checks

- Access token exists
- Token is valid
- Token not expired
- User exists
- User active

Failure Response

```json
{
  "success": false,
  "message": "Authentication required."
}
```

No business logic executes before authentication succeeds.

---

# Step 4 — Authorization

Purpose

Verify the user can perform the requested action.

Checks

- Organization membership
- Role permissions
- Feature access
- Subscription restrictions

Example

```text
Accountant
✓ Create Invoice

Viewer
✗ Create Invoice
```

Failure Response

```json
{
  "success": false,
  "message": "Insufficient permissions."
}
```

---

# Step 5 — Request Validation

Purpose

Verify request data.

Examples

- Required fields
- Data types
- Value ranges
- Business constraints

Example

```json
{
  "customerId": "required",
  "items": "must contain at least one item"
}
```

Validation occurs before business logic.

---

# Step 6 — Business Service Execution

Purpose

Execute business rules.

Example

Create Invoice Service

Checks

- Customer exists
- Products exist
- Organization active
- Invoice numbering rules

Responsibilities

- Business decisions
- Workflow execution
- Domain rule enforcement

Services should not contain HTTP-specific logic.

---

# Step 7 — Database Transaction

Purpose

Persist business data safely.

Operations

- Insert records
- Update records
- Create snapshots
- Generate audit references

Requirements

- Atomic
- Consistent
- Reliable
- Rollback on failure

Business-critical operations should use transactions.

---

# Step 8 — Domain Event Publication

Purpose

Notify the system that something important occurred.

Example

```text
Invoice Created
```

Publish Event

```text
INVOICE_CREATED
```

Event consumers may include:

- Notifications
- Audit Logs
- Analytics
- Integrations

The business operation should complete without waiting for these consumers.

---

# Step 9 — Response Generation

Purpose

Return a standardized response.

Success Example

```json
{
  "success": true,
  "data": {}
}
```

Failure Example

```json
{
  "success": false,
  "message": "Unable to create invoice."
}
```

Responses should never expose internal implementation details.

---

# Step 10 — Background Processing

Purpose

Handle non-critical operations asynchronously.

Examples

- Email delivery
- Notification creation
- Analytics updates
- PDF generation
- Webhook delivery

Background failures must not affect completed business operations.

---

# Request Context

Every request should carry context.

Required Context

- Request ID
- User ID
- Organization ID
- Branch ID (optional)
- IP Address
- User Agent
- Timestamp

Context should be available throughout the request lifecycle.

---

# Correlation IDs

Every request receives a unique identifier.

Example

```text
req_01ABCXYZ123
```

Benefits

- Easier debugging
- Log tracing
- Audit tracking
- Error investigation

All logs should include the correlation ID.

---

# Transaction Boundaries

Transactions should only contain:

- Business writes
- Critical validations

Transactions should not contain:

- Email sending
- Analytics
- Notification delivery
- External API calls

These belong to asynchronous processing.

---

# Error Handling

Errors should be handled consistently.

Examples

401 Unauthorized

Authorization Error

↓

403 Forbidden

Validation Error

↓

422 Validation Failed

Business Error

↓

409 Conflict

Unexpected Error

↓

500 Internal Server Error

---

# Observability

Every request should generate:

Structured Logs

Metrics

Audit Records (where applicable)

Performance Measurements

Examples

- API Duration
- Database Duration
- Queue Duration

---

# Request Lifecycle Rules

Every request must:

Pass authentication.

Pass authorization.

Pass validation.

Execute business logic.

Persist safely.

Publish events.

Return standardized responses.

Generate observability data.

# 18. Architecture Decision Records (ADR)

## Overview

Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) document significant architectural decisions made within InvoicePro.

Each ADR captures:

- The problem being solved
- The decision made
- Alternative options considered
- Trade-offs
- Expected consequences

ADRs preserve architectural knowledge and provide context for future engineers, architects, and decision makers.

Architectural decisions should be revisited only when new information or changing business requirements justify reconsideration.

---

# ADR-001

## Title

Modular Monolith Architecture

---

## Status

Approved

---

## Context

InvoicePro requires a scalable architecture that supports rapid product development while minimizing operational complexity.

The platform is currently in its early growth phase and must support future expansion without introducing unnecessary infrastructure overhead.

---

## Decision

InvoicePro will use a Modular Monolith Architecture.

Business domains will remain logically separated while being deployed as a single application.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Microservices

Rejected

### Traditional Layered Monolith

Rejected

---

## Reasoning

Benefits:

- Faster development
- Simpler deployment
- Easier debugging
- Lower infrastructure costs
- Easier testing

---

## Consequences

Positive

- High development velocity
- Lower operational burden

Negative

- Future service extraction may be required
